# Model card: demand-forecast-net

## Model details

- **Type:** global cross-series MLP with a negative-binomial head - a
  "DeepAR-lite without the RNN" (Salinas et al. 2020; global-model evidence
  from M4/M5). Implemented in plain PyTorch in
  [`mllab/demand_forecast_net/model.py`](../../mllab/demand_forecast_net/model.py).
- **Inputs:** per (SKU, month) - lags {1, 2, 3, 12, 13}, rolling means
  {3, 6, 12}, 6 Fourier month features (sin/cos, k = 1..3), and two causal
  Croston states (smoothed nonzero size and inter-demand interval, alpha = 0.1).
  16 features total, plus an 8-dim learned SKU embedding.
- **Architecture:** embedding (200 x 8) -> concat -> Linear 24-64 -> ReLU ->
  Linear 64-64 -> ReLU -> two softplus heads (NB mean `mu`, dispersion `r`).
  **7,490 trainable parameters** (1,600 of them the SKU embedding), counted
  from the instantiated module.
- **Training config:** full-batch Adam, lr 0.02, 400 epochs, seed 13,
  `torch.manual_seed` fixed; features standardized with training-set mean/std;
  loss is the NB negative log-likelihood **weighted per sample by inverse
  series scale** (normalized to mean 1) so training targets the scale-free
  MASE/RMSSE rather than the few high-volume SKUs. The model is retrained from
  scratch at each rolling origin on data strictly before that origin.
- **One improvement over the base method:** intermittent-demand handling -
  Croston smoothed size/interval states as extra causal features plus the
  inverse-scale loss weighting.

## Intended use

- Demonstrate that a small global model with lag/calendar features and a count
  likelihood can beat classical univariate baselines on short, partly
  intermittent monthly series - as a portfolio/method exhibit and a starting
  template for a real B2B demand pipeline.
- **Out of scope:** any real purchasing, inventory, or staffing decision. The
  model is trained only on synthetic data; it produces one-step-ahead monthly
  point forecasts (the NB mean) only - no multi-horizon path, no prediction
  intervals are surfaced by the CLI, and no real-data validation exists.

## Training data

- Synthetic, generated by `mllab.synth.make_demand_panel(seed=13)`: 200 SKUs x
  42 months. ~75% seasonal series (level ~ U(20, 200), sinusoidal annual
  seasonality with amplitude U(0.15, 0.6), mild linear trend, negative-binomial
  noise with dispersion r = 6) and ~25% intermittent series (occurrence
  probability U(0.15, 0.45), NB sizes around a base of U(2, 12), r = 3).
- **Assumptions baked in:** stationary seasonality with a fixed 12-month
  period, no promotions, no price effects, no stockouts/censoring, and
  independence across SKUs. Real demand data violates all of these.
- Deterministic: the same seed reproduces the same panel byte-for-byte (tests
  enforce this).

## Evaluation

- **Metrics: MASE and RMSSE** under one-step rolling-origin cross-validation
  (origins = months 39, 40, 41; per-series scaling denominators computed from
  history before the first origin). Why: with 200 series whose levels span
  roughly 2 to 200 units and many zeros, absolute errors (MAE/RMSE) are
  dominated by the high-volume SKUs and percentage errors (MAPE) explode on
  zeros; MASE/RMSSE are the M5-standard scale-free choices, and a value below
  1.0 means beating in-sample seasonal-naive.
- **Measured (seed 13, printed by `python -m mllab.demand_forecast_net`):**

  | Model | MASE | RMSSE |
  |---|---|---|
  | global NB MLP | **0.987** | **0.948** |
  | seasonal-naive | 1.080 | 1.062 |
  | Holt-Winters (additive) | 1.101 | 1.019 |

  The net beats both named baselines on both metrics. The margin is real but
  modest (~9% MASE vs seasonal-naive), which is the honest expectation for a
  small model on 42-month series.

## Limitations and failure modes

- **Cold-start blindness:** features require lag 13, so a SKU contributes no
  training rows and receives no forecast until it has 13 months of history
  (`MIN_T = 13` in `features.py`). New products - exactly where forecasting
  help is most wanted - are simply invisible to this model.
- **One-step only:** the rolling-origin evaluation is strictly one month
  ahead. Nothing here validates 3- or 12-month-ahead performance, and the lag
  features would have to be replaced by recursive or direct multi-horizon
  strategies, both of which degrade.
- **No exogenous signals:** promotions, price changes, and calendar events are
  absent from generator and features alike; on real data their omission would
  show up as systematic error spikes the model cannot explain.
- **Distribution head is trusted blindly:** the NB dispersion output is never
  evaluated (no coverage/backtest of intervals); only the mean is scored.
- **Seed-scoped evidence:** results are for seed 13; the test suite checks the
  model beats seasonal-naive, but the exact margins move with the seed.

## Ethical and deployment considerations

The direct subject of the forecasts is products, not people, so the ethical
surface is smaller than for the churn model - but not zero. Demand forecasts
drive purchasing and, downstream, warehouse staffing and supplier orders;
systematic under-forecasting of the intermittent (long-tail) SKUs would
quietly degrade service for exactly the small customers who depend on those
items, and the inverse-scale weighting that protects them here is a design
choice that should be re-justified, not assumed, on real data. Anyone
deploying a descendant of this model should monitor forecast bias separately
for high- and low-volume segments and keep a human review step on order
quantities until interval calibration has been demonstrated on live data.
