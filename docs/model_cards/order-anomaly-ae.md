# Model card: order-anomaly-ae

## Model details

- **Type:** reconstruction-error anomaly detection (Sakurada & Yairi 2014;
  Hawkins et al. 2002) with **two** scorers reported side by side:
  - an undercomplete autoencoder `d-16-4-16-d` (ReLU hidden, linear output),
    trained on normal orders only with a denoising perturbation
    ([`ae.py`](../../mllab/order_anomaly_ae/ae.py), **395 trainable
    parameters** at d = 7, counted from the instantiated module);
  - a **mandatory numpy PCA-SVD baseline** (top k = 4 components,
    torch-free, [`model.py`](../../mllab/order_anomaly_ae/model.py)).
  The anomaly score for both is the per-row squared reconstruction error in
  standardised feature space.
- **Training config (AE):** full-batch Adam, lr 1e-2, 300 epochs, seed 3,
  MSE loss, Gaussian input noise sigma = 0.1 (denoising), bottleneck 4.
  Standardisation is fit on the normal training split only.
- **Splits:** 60% of *normal* rows for training, 15% of normals as a clean
  validation split (used to set the alert threshold at the
  1 - contamination percentile, an explicit false-positive budget), and the
  remaining rows - 1,363 orders including all 150 anomalies - for evaluation.
- **One improvement over the base method:** the clean-split percentile
  threshold plus per-feature error attribution for every flag ("driven by
  customer_tenure (63%), lead_time_days (17%)" is the actual seed-3 output).

## Intended use

- Demonstrate honest anomaly-detector evaluation: a deep model is only worth
  reporting next to the simple baseline it must beat, and the CLI recommends
  whichever wins. Template for order/delivery screening (fat-finger
  quantities, discount abuse, broken price-quantity relationships) where
  flags go to a *human* reviewer.
- **Out of scope:** automated blocking or reversal of orders with no human in
  the loop; fraud *adjudication* (a reconstruction error is evidence of
  "unusual", not of "wrongful"); and any real deployment without re-deriving
  the contamination rate and threshold from that system's own verified-normal
  data.

## Training data

- Synthetic, generated by `mllab.synth.make_orders(seed=3)`: 5,000 orders x 7
  features (order_value, quantity, discount_pct, lead_time_days, unit_price,
  num_line_items, customer_tenure) drawn from a rank-3 factor model so the
  features are realistically correlated, then clipped to positive scales.
- **3% injected anomalies of three kinds:** extreme univariate (one feature
  blown up 6-10 sigma), broken correlation (large order value with
  independently redrawn low quantity), impossible combo (near-maximum
  discount on a tiny, expensive-unit order).
- **Assumptions baked in:** anomalies are rare (3%), mutually independent,
  and *not adversarial* - none of the injected kinds tries to stay close to
  the normal manifold. Labels are known exactly, which is never true in a
  real order stream.
- Deterministic under the seed (tests enforce this).

## Evaluation

- **Metrics: PR-AUC (primary), ROC-AUC, precision@k** (k = number of true
  anomalies in the eval set), with the assumed contamination rate reported
  alongside. Why: at 3% prevalence ROC-AUC is inflated by the easy 97% of
  true negatives; PR-AUC and P@k measure what the reviewer actually
  experiences - how much of the flag queue is real. All metrics are computed
  by the repo's own from-scratch implementations in
  [`mllab/metrics.py`](../../mllab/metrics.py).
- **Measured (seed 3, printed by `python -m mllab.order_anomaly_ae`;
  n = 1,363, anomalies = 150, contamination 0.030):**

  | Scorer | ROC-AUC | PR-AUC | P@k |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | PCA baseline (k=4) | 0.976 | 0.951 | 0.913 |
  | Autoencoder | **0.982** | **0.963** | **0.933** |

  The AE wins **narrowly** on this seed. The repo's own README states the
  policy plainly: on seeds where PCA wins, prefer PCA - the deep model does
  not earn its complexity by a 0.012 PR-AUC margin.

## Limitations and failure modes

- **The threshold assumes a verifiably clean validation split.** The
  percentile cut is taken on held-out *normal* data; in production nobody
  hands you guaranteed-clean orders. Contamination of that split drags the
  threshold upward and silently lowers recall. Order-mix drift (new customer
  segments, seasonal shifts) invalidates a fixed percentile cut over time; it
  must be re-estimated on a fresh verified-normal window at a fixed cadence.
- **The contamination rate is assumed known (3%)** because the generator sets
  it. In reality it is unknown and unstable, and both the threshold and the
  interpretation of P@k depend on it.
- **The flag explanation currently comes from the PCA reconstructor, not the
  AE:** `train.py` computes per-feature attribution via
  `PCAReconstructor.per_feature_error` even when the AE is the recommended
  scorer, so on seeds where the two models disagree about *why* a row is
  strange, the explanation shown may not match the score that flagged it.
- **Not adversarially robust:** injected anomalies are naive; a counterparty
  who keeps discount abuse *near* the normal correlation structure would
  score low by construction. The narrow AE-vs-PCA margin is also
  seed-dependent and should not be quoted as "AE beats PCA" in general.

## Ethical and deployment considerations

An anomaly flag on an order is easily read as an accusation - "possible
discount abuse" points at a salesperson or a customer. Reconstruction error
detects *statistical unusualness*, and unusual customers (new segments, small
foreign buyers, atypical but legitimate bulk deals) will be over-flagged
precisely because they are underrepresented in the "normal" training window;
that is a fairness problem as much as an accuracy problem. Deployment should
keep flags advisory, route them to human review with the per-feature
explanation attached, track false-positive rates per customer segment to
catch systematic over-flagging, and never feed raw flags into automated
consequences (order blocking, credit-limit changes) without a verified case.
